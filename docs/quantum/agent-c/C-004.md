## External oracle — genuine measured results

The test-only CSV includes complete gate sequences, basis indices and full complex amplitudes. The Qiskit verifier reconstructs each QuantumCircuit with native Qiskit operators and computes `Statevector.from_instruction`, comparing full complex vectors **without discounting global phase**; analytic Born probabilities and norm are separately checked. ## Scaling methodology and actual results

Code: `examples/scaling_bench.rs`, `cargo build --release`, four **separate** Linux runner processes n=4,8,12,16. For each: one unrecorded warmup and **nine timed repetitions**, each from a freshly allocated `|0...0>` pure state, then 64 gates and the simulator's per-gate invariant checks. Timing covers allocation + gate evolution + norm validations, using `Instant::now` and a final black-box norm. The ordering is fixed, not randomly scheduled. `min`, `median`, and **nearest-rank P95** (9 samples means P95 equals observed maximum) are recorded as microseconds. `VmHWM` is Linux **peak process-wide RSS**, including executable/runtime/system allocations; it does not isolate the statevector's allocation. Logical data bytes are `16 * 2^n` for f64 complex amplitudes. Environment and all source CSVs are attached to the workflow, not guessed.

The observed growth over each 4-qubit step is consistent with exponential statevector cost, but no universal complexity measurement or analytic speed-up theorem is inferred.
